# Gate 1 · Product

## The person problem

A grown-up wants to share a short dinosaur experience with a young child without handing
the child another noisy game or passive video. The child wants to look closely, choose what
interests them, and be heard. Both need a clear beginning and ending, language they can use
together, and confidence that the experience is calm and trustworthy.

## What success looks like

- The first screen makes it clear within ten seconds that the experience is for a child and
  grown-up to explore together.
- A child can point out at least one thing they notice without first being quizzed by the
  product.
- A grown-up can find and use one open-ended conversation prompt during a visit lasting no
  more than five minutes.
- The visit has an obvious, gentle ending that suggests one off-screen question or activity.
- In a small facilitated check, at least four of five child-and-grown-up pairs can complete
  the visit without instruction from the maker.
- The experience contains no account request, advertising, score, reward loop, automatic
  sound, behavioral tracking, or pressure to continue.

These outcomes are checked through direct, consent-based observation and conversation,
not through tracking children.

## Announcement

### Meet DUN's first prehistoric neighbor: Triceratops

DUN's first exhibit is open. Sit together, take a slow look at Triceratops, and follow the
details your child notices first. A few gentle prompts help grown-ups keep the conversation
going, then the visit ends with one question to carry into the rest of the day. There are
no points to earn and nothing starts playing by itself—just a quiet moment of discovery.

## Deliberately not doing

- No free-roaming museum or collection of multiple animals in this release.
- No quiz, correct-answer flow, score, badge, streak, timer, or reward animation.
- No narration, music, sound effects, or voice selection in this release.
- No account, saved progress, favorites, personalization, sharing, or social features.
- No open-ended chat with an AI character.
- No combat, hunting, roaring, jump scares, or dramatic chase scenes.
- No dense fact sheet intended for independent reading by a child.
- No attempt to keep the child on screen after the short visit ends.

## Proposed visit

1. The pair meets one Triceratops in a calm scene.
2. The child is invited to say or point to what they notice first.
3. The pair chooses from three observation prompts; none is scored.
4. A small number of carefully sourced facts can be revealed when the grown-up chooses.
5. The visit closes with one off-screen question and a clear return to the museum entrance.
